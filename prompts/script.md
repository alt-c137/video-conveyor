Structure:
1. Hook in the first 15 seconds, addressed to the viewer ("you"): a concrete moment from the viewer's own life + a twist or the hidden cost. It EXTENDS the curiosity of the title, it does not repeat the title. Plant one open question here that is only answered near the end.
2. Retention promise: what they will understand by the end.
3. {{sections}} meaning blocks. Each block = one clear idea, a concrete everyday example from the viewer's life, and it ends with a micro-cliffhanger into the next block.
4. A practical block: a simple, concrete method the viewer can use today (steps, a question to ask yourself, a rule).
5. Strong ending: conclusion / philosophical point that ties back to the hook.
6. Soft subscribe CTA in one or two sentences (and one short "if this is useful, subscribe" line right after the hook, starting with "Welcome back" or similar).

Retention craft (this is what keeps people watching):
- Every ~3 minutes a re-hook: a surprising fact, a question to the viewer, or a change of scale (one person -> a whole company/history).
- Link ideas with "but" and "so" (tension and consequence), never with "and then".
- Specific beats generic: a named situation, a number, a time of day, an object — not "many people often feel".
- Stakes: say what this bias costs the viewer (years, money, friendships, health) before you explain it.
- Payoff every promise you make. The ending answers the open question from the hook and calls back to its image.

Style:
- Short simple sentences for AI voiceover. No sentence longer than ~22 words. No brackets, no stage directions, no emojis, no lists with symbols inside the narration.
- Images that are easy to illustrate (objects, situations, gestures, places). Recurring metaphors (2-4) that come back through the video.
- Numbers and studies: only real, verifiable ones (researcher + year). Disputed points: exactly one honest hedge, then confident.
- No repetition of the same thought in different words. No filler ("in this video we will...", "let's dive in").
