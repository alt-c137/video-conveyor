Structure:
1. Hook (first 30-40 seconds) — the pattern of the faceless channels that win this niche (Nous Psychology, The Gen X Way, PsychToons, Throttle Psychology: 200-800k views on 10-30k-sub channels):
   a) Open INSIDE one hyper-specific present-tense scene the viewer recognises from their own life, with 3-5 sensory details nobody else would mention ("His knuckles are scraped. There is grease under his fingernails that will not fully wash out."; "They know exactly how hard to close the driver's side door."). "You know that person who..." or "There is a man in his garage right now." No setup, no context, no greeting.
   b) Name what everyone assumes about this person ("stubborn", "cheap", "reckless", "boring") and REJECT it: "This is not stubbornness. Not exactly." Defend the viewer — they must feel seen and respected, never judged.
   c) Promise something deeper and more interesting: "Something older, quieter and far more interesting is going on underneath." Plant one open question that is answered only near the end.
   d) Go STRAIGHT into the first idea. It EXTENDS the title's curiosity, never repeats the title.
2. NO channel intro, NO "welcome back", NO subscribe ask and NO agenda ("today you will learn 1, 2, 3", "by the end you will know") anywhere in the first 3 minutes. This block made viewers leave at 0:30-1:10 on our channel (lesson 2026-10-29).
3. {{sections}} meaning blocks. Each block = one clear idea, a concrete everyday example from the viewer's life, and it ends with a micro-cliffhanger into the next block.
4. A practical block: a simple, concrete method the viewer can use today (steps, a question to ask yourself, a rule).
5. Strong ending: conclusion / philosophical point that ties back to the hook.
6. Soft subscribe CTA: ONE sentence, only after a real payoff (around the middle, e.g. right after the first "aha" of the types/reasons block), plus one at the very end. Never before minute 3.

Retention craft (this is what keeps people watching):
- Every ~3 minutes a re-hook: a surprising fact, a question to the viewer, or a change of scale (one person -> a whole company/history).
- Link ideas with "but" and "so" (tension and consequence), never with "and then".
- Specific beats generic: a named situation, a number, a time of day, an object — not "many people often feel".
- Stakes: say what this bias costs the viewer (years, money, friendships, health) before you explain it.
- Identity, not lecture: the viewer is the hero. Frame findings as "people like you" with dignity (patience, competence, independence) — that is why the winners in the niche get shared.
- Payoff every promise you make. The ending answers the open question from the hook and calls back to its image.

Style:
- Short simple sentences for AI voiceover. No sentence longer than ~22 words. No brackets, no stage directions, no emojis, no lists with symbols inside the narration.
- Images that are easy to illustrate (objects, situations, gestures, places). Recurring metaphors (2-4) that come back through the video.
- Numbers and studies: only real, verifiable ones (researcher + year). Disputed points: exactly one honest hedge, then confident.
- No repetition of the same thought in different words. No filler ("in this video we will...", "let's dive in").
